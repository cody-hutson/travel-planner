# Interview conduct — how `/trip-record interview` asks and records

**This file is `/trip-record interview`'s conduct, authored here and nowhere else in this command.** `skills/trip-record/SKILL.md` § `## interview <form> [<target>]` is the verb — its signature, what it reads, the forms it admits, where the file lives, what it refuses and the standing rule its writes are taken under — and it binds the verb, and `## profile <name>` wherever that section runs the interview inline, to follow what is written here from the first question to the last write. That section settles, once per invocation and before the session runs, which form, which file, whether to create or resume and what it refuses, and it names the standing rule the writes are taken under; this file governs the session itself — what is asked, how, and how each answer is recorded, write by write, each write's own test included.

**What this file does not do.** It declares no read: every file the rules below read is named on that section's `**Reads:**` line, which is the verb's read scope, so a read named only here would be outside it. It names no field of any form: every field the interview touches is read from the form it is given. Every engine path named here is repository-relative to the engine root, as `skills/trip-record/SKILL.md` states for every document opened from it. A bare rule number here names one of the rules in § *The rules every interview shares*; a standing rule of the command is always named as a standing rule, and a rule only a write-capable session keeps carries a `W`.

## The question set — read from the form, never from the file

The question set is the form's, so a line deleted from the file by hand never retires a question. On a form with a boundary it is: the title line's bracketed placeholder; every `##` section above the boundary, in order; under each, its labelled bullets — `- `, an optional star, then the bold label and its colon — and any unlabelled free-text line it carries, in order. **A field's section is the `Section` value its enclosing `##` heading resolves to under the rule `reference/data-model.md` § *The denominator* states, and its `Scope` and class are those of the one row of `reference/data-model.md` § *Field Scope* → *The classification* it joins on `(section, label)`** — read live, never restated. Every labelled bullet joins exactly one row by the time a question is asked, because the verb refuses a form where one does not. A `block`-scoped label belongs to a repeat unit: the run of labelled bullets the form repeats, which the form ships already seeded. A field the classification says is never asked is not asked, and its line is left as the form ships it. Nothing below the boundary is a question, whatever it looks like. A block-owned form's question set is the one § *On a block-owned form* below states.

## The rules every interview shares

These bind any channel an interview runs in, whether or not it can write the file. They are numbered so that another carrier of the same rules can be compared with them rule by rule. `templates/interview-card.md` is that carrier: it gives these rules, word for word, to an assistant with no repository, and `scripts/test-artifact-schema.sh` group `PC` fails the moment the two texts differ.

1. **One section at a time, in the form's order.** Settle the current section before the next, and never put the whole form in front of them at once. The order is a default for your next question and never a constraint on them: someone who asks to go to a named section is taken there.
2. **Two or three questions a section at most, in plain words.** A bracketed placeholder is a hint for you, never a script to read out.
3. **Offer only the choices the form carries.** Where a field's bracketed placeholder carries options, offer those, quoted, taking the text after any `closed:` or `open:` head — the head is a note about the list and never a member of it. A section's guidance quote, its menu or tick list, the ask-prose below the boundary and the worked example are not a field's option source. Where a field's placeholder carries no options, ask it open and offer none; an example the ask-prose gives for its section may still be mentioned as an example, never offered as a choice (ADR-022 D5.3; ADR-023 D2.3, D2.4).
4. **A closed field records a member the answer names, or the un-answer the answer chose.** Where the placeholder opens with `closed:`, record the member their words contain, or an unambiguous pick by position; where their words name no member, say so and offer the members again, and never choose the nearest. On a closed field, offer only the members the placeholder lists, and never an example it carries as a choice of its own. The em dash, and a word the form declares in its place, stay admissible on a closed field as on any other. A field opening with `open:` records their words, whether or not they match a suggestion (ADR-022 D5.4).
5. **The starred fields first, then a decision that is theirs.** Ask the fields the form marks with a star before the rest. A starred field inside a repeat unit brings its unit with it: once it is answered, ask the rest of that unit's labels before anything outside the unit, so no unit is left part-answered when the starred fields are done. When the last of them is settled and anything else remains, say what is left and offer exactly three branches — keep going, stop here, or go to a named section — each of them a complete answer (ADR-022 D3.1, D2.2).
6. **A skip is heard, never inferred.** When they skip a field, take the skip and move on, and never ask that field again. Silence is neither a skip nor assent: on silence or an unclear reply, offer once more — answer it, skip it, or stop — and take nothing until they choose; a second silence ends the session (ADR-022 D5.5; ADR-026 § 5).
7. **Never invent.** Take only what they said. A confirmation may contain only words they said in this turn, the field's own label as the form spells it, and words that carry no value; strike the label and those words, and what is left must be a subsequence of what they said. Beyond the options rule 3 admits, a prompt may name a field and a remedy, and never carries, quotes or offers a candidate value — a value arrived at rather than one the form lists. Never fill a field with a plausible guess, and never infer one field from another (ADR-022 D5.1; ADR-026 § 5).
8. **Keep their words.** Tightening may delete words and never adds one. The single exception is the narrowing a form states for a field that covers its own subject alone; the form's ask-prose names it, and no other field admits a narrowing (ADR-022 D5.2, D4.4).
9. **On doubt, ask.** If you are not sure what they meant, ask, and never paraphrase your way past it (ADR-022 D5.6).
10. **A field the form computes is never asked**, and its line is left as the form ships it (ADR-022 D3.5).
11. **An answer that belongs on another form is named there and not recorded here.** Name the other form and the field; carry, quote and offer no value — they say it again there (ADR-022 D4.3).
12. **The person the form is about, and no one else.** Ask about someone else only where the form itself asks about the people travelling with them, and never for another person's identity details (ADR-022 D4.4).
13. **A `closed:` or `open:` head is never written into an answer.** An answer someone wrote by hand that kept its head is their answer, and is left as they wrote it (ADR-023 D2.4).
14. **A form's own conduct stays with the form.** The ask-prose below a form's boundary says how to ask each of its sections and what that form does differently — which field it asks first, which answers belong on another form, which word stands in for the em dash. Follow it. Where it and a rule above differ on what may be said, offered or recorded, the rule governs. A rule number inside a form's ask-prose is that form's own, and never one of these.

## What only a write-capable session adds

These bind a session that holds a write path to the file — `/trip-record interview`, and `## profile <name>` running it inline. A channel that can return text but cannot write is the no-repository route's, and it keeps the rules above and not these.

- **W1 — The file is the session.** Nothing is held in conversation that the file does not hold: the create branch writes the form before the first question, a single field is written when it is answered, and a repeat unit when it is complete — the one span a session holds that the file does not yet (ADR-022 D1.1, D1.2, D2.2, D6.1).
- **W2 — Resume by what the file says.** Ask the fields the file leaves un-asked or indeterminate, in the form's order, and never ask again one it answers or declines (ADR-022 D1.3, D1.4).
- **W3 — One answer, one write.** A `slot`-scoped field is written when it is answered, with `Edit`, changing only that field's lines — its bullet and the lines that continue it, as `reference/data-model.md` § *The interview's read of an unstated value* bounds a value — under standing rule 2's second condition. A repeat unit is written **whole** when it is complete, every label present, and a label of a completed unit that was skipped takes the em dash; a label the classification says is never asked is left as the form ships it. A unit already in the file is written in place with `Edit`, at unit granularity; a unit beyond the ones the form seeded is appended under standing rule 7. When they stop inside a unit before it is complete, say which of its answers and skips the file does not hold: nothing of an unfinished unit is written, and no label of it takes the em dash, because a label nobody asked was not skipped (ADR-022 D2.1, D2.2).
- **W4 — A spare unit is em-dashed and kept.** A unit nobody reaches keeps its brackets; a unit they decline takes the em dash on each of its labels; a unit they ask to have taken out is named, **echoed verbatim first**, and removed alone, never a whole section (ADR-022 D3.3, D3.6).
- **W5 — Changing an answer is one mechanism.** They name the field; echo the value the file holds now, verbatim; `Edit` only that field's lines; confirm. It is the same within a session and across sessions (ADR-022 D2.3).
- **W6 — Read the line before you write it.** A field's current line is read again in the same turn as its write. Where the file differs from what the session believes, the file prevails: say what it holds, and write nothing until they choose (ADR-022 D2.6).
- **W7 — A skip is the em dash, written before the next question — for a label inside a repeat unit, with its unit (W3).** Never write the em dash on a turn in which they said nothing. A surviving bracket means nobody has asked, and nothing here writes a bracket back (ADR-022 D2.2, D2.4, D5.5).
- **W8 — A relayed answer is marked.** An answer the operator gives on the subject's behalf carries `[OPERATOR-PROVIDED]`, at field granularity, and is written only where the target class admits that mark — read from the class's own schema. Where the class does not admit it, the answer is not written: name the field and the reason, and no value. When the subject later gives a different value, the mark goes in the same write (ADR-022 D4.1, D4.2).
- **W9 — The frontmatter is written once.** The file's creation writes it whole — this verb's create branch, where the form admits one — and no later write touches it, so a partly filled file conforms at every instant (ADR-022 D2.5).
- **W10 — A spoken session treats the person-class questions with care.** Before the first question whose field the classification types `PERSON`, say once that the next questions cover health- or document-shaped detail, and offer to move them to text or to leave them for later; in speech, a confirmation of such a field names the field and asks for assent without saying the value again (ADR-022 D6.3).

## On a block-owned form

A form declaring `form-version: 2` has no boundary and no single writer: its writer table decides, region by region, whether this command may write there, and `reference/region-reference.md` carries that decision already made for every region of the form. `skills/trip-record/SKILL.md` § `## interview <form> [<target>]` settles before the first question that the file covers the form; this section is how the session uses it. Everything above binds such a form too, read as this section says.

**Where a field is, and what its region gives.** A field's region is the row of the region reference, among the rows for this form, whose line is the greatest not after the field's own line in the form and whose extent reaches it — the field's line in the form, never in the file, because an instance renames its repeat headings. Read three cells of that row: its verdict, its row's `Block` cell, and, on a `CONDITIONAL` region, the condition under which it holds for this command. The title line, and a line above the form's first region, are in no region. Nothing here resolves a region itself, and a field whose region the file does not give is refused.

**The pre-write test, before every write.** The field's region must read `YES`, or `CONDITIONAL` with its condition holding now. A condition is read over the states `ADR-024` decision 1 admits: whether the file exists at the moment of the write, and the lifecycle the trip resolved to. This verb never creates a block-owned file, so a condition that holds once the file exists holds for every write it makes, and one that holds only at the file's creation holds for none; a condition that is neither is undecidable, and gives this command nothing (`ADR-024` R4). Otherwise nothing is written, and the refusal names the field, its region's heading, the row that region reached by its `Block` cell as the region reference carries it, and the verdict that denied it, and carries no value. An answer for anything in no region is refused the same way, naming that no region of the form holds it. **Where the target is the title line, or sits in a block that `## fact <statement>`'s ownership route gives to another verb of this command, the refusal also names that verb once** — the title line is `destination`'s — so no refusal leaves the operator at a field this command writes elsewhere.

**Which verb of this command records the write — `## fact <statement>`'s, run inline.** The writer table names commands and not verbs, so the split inside this command is this command's own, and `## fact <statement>` states it in its ownership route: a block that route gives to another verb of this command is that verb's, and the rest are its own. **Every answer taken on such a form is recorded by that section's procedure**, run inline in this verb's own turn and dispatching nothing — its ownership route, its write shapes, its link-don't-copy and third-party bounds, and the cross-block consequences it names and never performs — with the answer as the statement and the field as where it lands, cited and never restated. That keeps that section's own claim true: inside this command, the blocks it keeps are written by its procedure alone. **A block that route gives to another verb of this command is not asked here, and an answer volunteered for one is not recorded here**: this verb names that verb once, carries no value, and the operator states the answer there. Where that procedure and this file differ, this file governs the cadence, the skip, the pre-write re-read and the one narrowing `ADR-022` D2.2 makes to that procedure's unit shape — which labels of a completed unit take the em dash, as W3 states — and that procedure governs everything else.

**The question set on such a form** is every labelled bullet, at any indent, in a region the pre-write test admits and whose block that route keeps for its own procedure, in the form's order; each joins its classification row on `(section, label)` exactly as any form's does. A region's unlabelled lines are never asked, because the key the question set joins on cannot address a line without a label. That includes a line carrying a bracketed answer another block is computed from — on the trip-context form, each journey leg's flight line: it is not asked, and an answer volunteered for it is recorded by that procedure's fill shape on that line, as any answer in an admitted region is. Nothing outside a region is ever asked.

**How the rules above read on such a form — the statement `ADR-022` R4 requires, and none of it a new decision.**

- **Shared rule 5.** Such a form may star nothing. Its decision point is then reached before the first question — every starred field is settled, vacuously — so the session says what is left and offers its three branches there.
- **Conformance at every write (`ADR-022` D2.5).** On a form whose output class is validated, as trip-context's is, the version-0 skip that D2.5's third ground rests on is not available; its first ground then carries the claim alone, because the validator's declared scope reaches that class's frontmatter and none of its body, and this verb never writes the frontmatter or the `intake-form` block, which the file's creation wrote (W9).
- **Shared rule 12, and who is interviewed.** The subject is the trip, and the person answering is the operator — the writer the table names through this command. No answer is given on another person's behalf, so W8 is not engaged, and a question about a person is asked only where the form itself asks it.
- **W10.** It is read from the classification, as on any form. Where the classification types no field of the form `PERSON`, its care does not engage: the form's health-shaped fields are the trip's, answered by the operator, and confirmed as any field is.
- **Shared rule 14.** Such a form has no boundary and nothing below one, so its own conduct is the guidance each region this verb may write quotes.
