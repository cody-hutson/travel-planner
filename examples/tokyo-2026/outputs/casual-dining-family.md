# Casual Dining — Family Subgroup (Sam, Pat, Riley)
**Trip:** Jul 15-22 | **Base:** Ginza Riverside Residence, Ginza Shintomicho
**Budget:** Y1,000-3,000/person | **Preference:** Food courts, depachika, food halls, stalls, casual izakaya | **No raw fish pressure, all cooked options**

> This guide provides food-court-style alternatives for the family subgroup on days when
> the main itinerary meal may be too formal, too adventurous, or when the group splits.

## Day 1 — Wed Jul 15 (Arrival Evening)

The itinerary already has **Yurakucho Under the Tracks** (yakitori, Y2,000-4,000) as the default. That IS the casual option and works perfectly for everyone. But if the family is too jet-lagged for smoky outdoor stalls in July heat:

### Option 1A: Mitsukoshi Ginza — Depachika B2
- **Type:** Department store food basement (depachika)
- **Location:** 4-6-16 Ginza, Chuo-ku — 4-min walk from hotel
- **Hours:** 10:00 AM - 8:00 PM (food basement typically until 8 PM)
- **Open Wednesday:** Yes
- **Price:** Y500-2,000 to assemble a full meal
- **What to get:** Pre-made bento boxes (tonkatsu bento, chicken teriyaki bento, tempura bento), wagyu beef croquettes from the hot counter, grilled fish sets, onigiri assortment. The depachika bento is a complete meal for Y800-1,500.
- **Why it works for arrival:** Buy and take back to the hotel room. Eat at the kitchenette. Zero decision fatigue. AC the whole time. The food quality in a Ginza depachika is better than most restaurants.
- **Walk from hotel:** 4 minutes

### Option 1B: Ginza Six — Food Basement B2
- **Type:** Department store food basement (depachika), upscale
- **Location:** 6-10-1 Ginza, Chuo-ku — 5-min walk from hotel
- **Hours:** Food floor 10:30 AM - 8:30 PM
- **Open Wednesday:** Yes
- **Price:** Y600-2,500 per person
- **What to get:** Similar to Mitsukoshi but slightly more curated — prepared bento from name-brand kitchen counters, seasonal sweets, grilled skewers. The Nakaei stand does excellent prepared sushi packs if anyone wants that; the cooked option people can get tonkatsu or karaage bento.
- **Why it works for arrival:** Same take-back-to-hotel play. Ginza Six is a newer, more modern building — the depachika layout is more open and easier to navigate when exhausted.
- **Walk from hotel:** 5 minutes

**Recommendation for Day 1:** The Yurakucho yakitori is the right call if energy allows — it is casual, it is a food stall experience, and everything is grilled. If energy does not allow, send someone to Mitsukoshi B2 with a Y5,000 note and buy bento for the group. Hotel room dinner. No shame.

The itinerary has **Kagari Ramen** (Y1,200-1,800) as the main lunch. Kagari is excellent and casual. But if the family wants something with more variety and less queue:

### Option 2B: Tsukiji Outer Market — Cooked Food Stalls
- **Type:** Market food stalls (covered)
- **Location:** 10-min walk from hotel — already visiting for breakfast
- **Hours:** Most stalls 7:00 AM - 2:00 PM (some to 3 PM)
- **Open Thursday:** Yes — Thursday is a prime market day
- **Price:** Y500-1,500 per person
- **What to get (all cooked):** Tamagoyaki skewers (Y200-400), grilled scallops (Y500-800), grilled wagyu-on-a-stick, crab croquettes, chicken yakitori, takoyaki (octopus balls — fully cooked despite the octopus), taiyaki for dessert. The covered inner lanes have partial shade.

The itinerary offers **Ginza Lion Beer Hall** (Y1,500-3,500), **Ichiran Ramen** (Y900-1,500), or **Tsukishima Monjayaki** (Y1,500-3,000). All three are genuinely casual. For the family specifically:

### Option 2C: Ginza Lion Beer Hall
- **Type:** Historic beer hall (Japan's oldest, est. 1934)
- **Location:** 7-9-20 Ginza — 5-min walk from hotel
- **Hours:** 11:30 AM - 11:00 PM
- **Open Thursday:** Yes
- **Price:** Y1,500-3,500 per person
- **What to order:** Hamburg steak (Japanese-style hamburger patty), fried shrimp (ebi fry), Napolitan spaghetti (ketchup-based pasta, a uniquely Japanese comfort food), draft Sapporo. Everything is cooked yoshoku (Western-influenced Japanese comfort food).

- **Open Thursday:** Yes
- **Price:** Y1,500-3,000 per person

- **Why for the family:** Interactive, social, nothing raw, and the cook-it-yourself format means continuous eating at your own pace. The whole street is monja restaurants — just walk until you find one with seats. Pat will enjoy the activity of cooking together.

The itinerary has **Harmonica Yokocho** in Kichijoji (Y800-1,500) and **Maisen Tonkatsu** in Omotesando (Y1,500-3,000). Both are casual. But if the family wants a proper food court experience during the Shibuya/Harajuku shopping afternoon:

### Option 4A: Shibuya Hikarie — ShinQs Food Floor (B2-B3)
- **Type:** Department store food hall / depachika
- **Location:** Shibuya Hikarie building, directly connected to Shibuya Station — 0-min walk from station
- **Hours:** B2-B3 food floors 10:00 AM - 9:00 PM
- **Open Saturday:** Yes
- **Price:** Y800-2,000 per person

- **Why for the family:** Shibuya Hikarie is the first building at the station when arriving from Kichijoji via Keio Inokashira Line. Before the group fans out to SONIANDSMI and PARCO, the family can eat in the food court while the shoppers grab a quick bowl. AC, seating, variety, no decision paralysis.
- **Walk from Shibuya Station:** Connected directly (2-min walk through the station corridors)

### Option 4B: Shibuya PARCO — Food Floor (B1)
- **Type:** Food court / restaurant floor
- **Location:** Shibuya PARCO B1 — the group is already going here for Pokemon Center (6F) and Sylvanian Families (5F)
- **Hours:** 11:00 AM - 9:00 PM (restaurant floor)
- **Open Saturday:** Yes
- **Price:** Y900-2,500 per person
- **What to get:** The B1 food floor has a curated selection: ramen shops, curry specialists, set-meal (teishoku) counters, a good tonkatsu spot. It is a food court in format — order at a counter, get a buzzer, sit at shared tables.
- **Why for the family:** You are literally already in the building for shopping. Go to B1 before going to 5F/6F, or after. Zero additional transit. The food is a step above typical food-court quality because PARCO curates its tenants.

### Option 4C: Harajuku Gyoza-Ro
- **Type:** Gyoza specialist counter
- **Location:** Harajuku back street, near Takeshita-dori — near the itinerary's shopping corridor
- **Hours:** 11:30 AM - 3:00 AM (varies)
- **Open Saturday:** Yes
- **Price:** Y800-1,200 per person
- **What to order:** Gyoza. That is the only thing on the menu. Crispy-bottomed, juicy pork dumplings. Order a plate (6 pieces, Y290-400), then another plate, then another. Add rice. Everything is cooked.
- **Why for the family:** Cheap, fast, completely approachable, and near the Harajuku shopping corridor (Kiddy Land is steps away). Standing/counter format — in and out in 20 minutes. Riley will eat six plates.
- **Note:** Cash only. Standing/counter seating only — check if Pat is OK standing for 15-20 min.

## Day 5 — Sun Jul 19 (Asakusa / Toyosu)

### LUNCH (Asakusa area, late morning)

### Option 5A: Nakamise-dori and Asakusa Side Streets — Street Food
- **Type:** Food stalls / street vendors along the temple approach
- **Location:** Senso-ji temple area, Asakusa — the group is already here for the morning temple visit
- **Hours:** Most stalls 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM (some to 6 PM)
- **Open Sunday:** Yes — Sunday is one of the busiest days
- **Price:** Y500-1,500 per person grazing
- **What to get (all cooked):** Freshly fried melon pan (sweet bread), age-manju (deep-fried sweet bean cakes — Asakusa specialty, try Asakusa Kuremutsu), senbei (grilled rice crackers — watch them press and grill in front of you), kaminari okoshi (traditional Asakusa thunder cracker candy), dango (grilled rice dumplings with sweet soy glaze), nikuman (steamed pork buns).
- **Why for the family:** The stall-grazing format means everyone picks what they want. Everything is cooked. The Nakamise approach to Senso-ji is covered (rain or sun protected). This is the quintessential Japanese food stall experience and it is already on the walking route.
- **Heat note:** The covered Nakamise arcade provides shade. Side streets do not — retreat to AC shops between stalls.

### Option 5B: Asakusa Underground Shopping Street (Asakusa Chikagai)
- **Type:** Retro underground food alley
- **Location:** Directly under Asakusa Station (Tobu/Metro), connected to the station exits
- **Hours:** Shops generally 10:00 AM - 8:00 PM
- **Open Sunday:** Yes
- **Price:** Y700-1,500 per person
- **What to get:** This is Tokyo's oldest underground shopping street (1955). Small lunch counters serving tempura bowls, curry rice, soba, udon, tonkatsu sets. The format is counter seating, point-and-order, fast.
- **Why for the family:** Full AC (it is underground). Retro atmosphere Pat will appreciate. Genuinely cheap. Right at the station — eat before heading to TeamLab. The food is simple home-cooking style (teishoku format).
- **Note:** Some shops may be dated and slightly run-down. That is the charm. The food is honest.

## Notes on Hours Verification

Hours listed are based on standard published operating hours for each venue. For the July trip, confirm the following before traveling:

- **Marine Day (Jul 20):** National holiday. Department stores and food halls are OPEN (they often close the next day, Tue Jul 21, as a substitute rest day — but Mitsukoshi Ginza and Ginza Six typically stay open). Verify closer to the date.
- **Wednesday closures (Jul 15 arrival, Jul 22 departure):** Some Tsukiji stalls close Wednesday. Mitsukoshi/Ginza Six depachika are open Wednesday.
- **Asakusa Chikagai:** Some stalls in the underground have irregular hours. Confirm before relying on this as the only lunch plan.
- **Shibuya Hikarie/PARCO:** Major commercial buildings with consistent hours. Reliable on Saturdays.
- **Ginza Bairin:** Established since 1927, consistent hours. Closed irregularly — check before going.
- **Depachika evening discounts:** The 20-50% markdowns on prepared food typically start 1-2 hours before closing. At Mitsukoshi (closes 8 PM), look for discount stickers from 6:30 PM onward.

## The Depachika Strategy (For Pat)

The single best casual-dining strategy for the family subgroup is the depachika. Three reasons:

1. **AC sanctuary.** Department store basements are climate-controlled year-round. In July Tokyo heat, this matters more than anything.
2. **No language barrier.** Everything is displayed, priced, and visible. Point, pay, eat. No ordering in Japanese required.
3. **Infinite variety at low cost.** A Y1,500 depachika meal assembles from 3-4 counters: a croquette here, a rice ball there, a grilled fish stick, a wagashi for dessert. Everyone gets exactly what they want.

The family's home-base depachika is **Mitsukoshi Ginza B2** (4-min walk from hotel). Use it as the default fallback for any meal when the planned restaurant does not appeal or energy is low. It never requires a reservation, it is always open (except Jan 1), and the food is always good.
