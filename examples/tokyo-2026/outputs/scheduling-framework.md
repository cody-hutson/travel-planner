## The First Thing I Need You to Understand: The Jet Lag Math

CDT to JST is +14 hours. That's not a minor adjustment — that's nearly a full day flip.

When your group lands at **4:00 PM JST on July 15**, their bodies believe it is **2:00 AM** the night before. They have been awake through what feels like an entire night. The adrenaline of arrival will carry them for maybe 90 minutes. Then it's a wall.

Here's what this means for your energy arc across the full trip:

| Day | Date | Jet Lag State | Energy Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jul 15 (Wed) | Arrival — bodies at 2 AM | 🔴 Survival |
| 2 | Jul 16 (Thu) | False dawn — wake at 4 AM, crash by noon | 🟠 Fragile |
| 3 | Jul 17 (Fri) | Turning the corner | 🟡 Building |
| 4 | Jul 18 (Sat) | **Peak window opens** | 🟢 Full capacity |
| 5 | Jul 19 (Sun) | Peak continues | 🟢 Full capacity |
| 6 | Jul 20 (Mon) | First hint of trip fatigue | 🟡 Strong but selective |
| 7 | Jul 21 (Tue) | Wind-down mode | 🟠 Comfortable, not ambitious |
| 8 | Jul 22 (Wed) | Departure morning | 🔴 Logistics only |

**Days 4 and 5 are your prime real estate.** Whatever you want most from this trip — a significant restaurant, a far destination, the experience that requires full presence — it goes there.

## The Heat Model (Non-Negotiable for This Group)

July in Tokyo with a heat-sensitive member means you're not scheduling *around* the heat — you're **engineering every day in three thermal zones:**

```
ZONE 1 — GREEN WINDOW:   8:00–11:00 AM    → Outdoor activity permitted
ZONE 2 — RED ZONE:       11:00 AM–5:00 PM → Indoor required (AC malls, dept stores, museums, underground passages)
ZONE 3 — AMBER EVENING:  5:00–7:30 PM     → Outdoor possible with monitoring
ZONE 4 — EVENING:        7:30 PM+         → Outdoor comfortable for dinner, strolling
```

The partner's mother should never be standing in direct sun between 11 AM and 5 PM. A heat index of 100°F+ for someone heat-sensitive in a city with no shade and radiant heat bouncing off pavement is a genuine safety issue.

The good news: **Tokyo is one of the most thermally engineered cities on earth.** Department stores, underground malls, museum complexes, covered shotengai shopping streets — you can spend 6 hours in midday Tokyo without ever stepping outside if you know the infrastructure. We'll use it deliberately.

## Daily Time-Block Template

Every day should follow this rough architecture, adjusted by energy level and location:

```
06:30–08:00   Light breakfast / convenience store run / slow start
08:00–11:00   ✅ OUTDOOR BLOCK — temples, markets, gardens, neighborhoods
11:00–11:30   Transit to midday refuge
11:30–14:30   🏬 INDOOR BLOCK — department store, museum, covered market, lunch
14:30–16:00   Return to hotel / rest / nap (especially Days 1–3)
16:00–17:30   Freshen up, transition
17:30–19:00   🌆 EVENING OUTDOOR — neighborhood stroll, golden hour
19:00–21:30   Dinner
```

> **Note:** The rest window at 14:30–16:00 is not optional on Days 1–3. It's what keeps the partner's mother functioning on Day 5 and what keeps everyone from snapping at each other on Day 6.

## Day-by-Day Structural Logic

### Day 1 — Jul 15 (Wed): The Arrival Protocol
**Energy: 🔴 Survival mode**

Here's what actually happens on arrival day from the Americas:
- Clear customs/immigration at NRT: 45–90 minutes
- Narita Express to Shimbashi/Ginza: ~1 hour
- Check-in, settle, unpack: 45 minutes
- **You are at a restaurant by 7:30–8:00 PM at earliest**

At that point, your group has been traveling 20+ hours and it's functionally 6 AM their time.

**The move: Do almost nothing.**

Walk the 8 minutes to Tsukiji Outer Market area — not to explore it properly, just to get your bearings and find dinner. Ramen, sushi, a reliable izakaya. Convenience store run for breakfast supplies and beer. Back to the hotel by 9:30 PM. Everyone in bed by 10.

Do not attempt Ginza at night. Do not attempt any "since we're already up" adventures. The version of this group that tries to push through Day 1 is the version that has a meltdown at 11 AM on Day 2 when they're standing in a temple in 95°F heat and nobody has eaten properly.

**No reservations needed. No advance planning required. Just land.**

---

### Day 2 — Jul 16 (Thu): The False Dawn
**Energy: 🟠 Fragile — treat with care**

Everyone will wake between 4:00–5:30 AM. This is not a problem. This is an opportunity.

**Tsukiji Outer Market** is one of the best 7:00–9:00 AM activities in Tokyo. It's walkable from your hotel, atmospheric in the early morning before the heat builds, and structured around *moving and eating* rather than sustained exertion. Tamagoyaki, fresh sushi breakfast, the sensory overload does the work of waking people up gently.

Be back at or near the hotel by 11 AM. The afternoon will likely bring a crash — especially for the partner's mother and anyone who didn't sleep well. This is **a neighborhood day.** Ginza covered arcades, a depachika exploration at Mitsukoshi, air-conditioned wandering. Nothing requiring trains to a far neighborhood.

**Day 2 is not the day for Teamlab. Day 2 is not the day for Asakusa. Day 2 is the day you let Tokyo come to you.**

---

### Day 3 — Jul 17 (Fri): Building Momentum
**Energy: 🟡 First real day**

By Day 3 you should have had two nights of actual sleep. This is when to schedule your **first "real" neighborhood excursion** — somewhere meaningful but not logistically punishing.

**Structural recommendation: Asakusa + Ueno corridor.** It's a direct shot from your hotel on the Hibiya Line (Hatchobori → Ueno, one transfer). Hit Senso-ji in the 8–10 AM window before the heat peaks, move into Ueno for a museum during the midday red zone, and be back well before dinner.

Friday evening is also your first proper izakaya evening — the city feels alive on Friday nights. A **dinner reservation here** works well if you have one that's moderately hard to get but not your crown jewel meal.

---

### Day 4 — Jul 18 (Sat): Prime Day #1
**Energy: 🟢 Full capacity — deploy your big card**

This is where your most ambitious or most desired experience goes. Saturday also means the city is fully alive — Shibuya, Harajuku, Omotesando are buzzing.

**Structural suggestion: West side of the city — Harajuku/Omotesando/Shibuya.** These neighborhoods are thermally manageable because they're dense with covered shopping (Omotesando Hills, Tokyu Hands, Shibuya Scramble Square, etc.) and the streets are well-shaded. The outdoor window (8–11 AM) is excellent for Meiji Jingu or the Omotesando streetscape before it gets oppressive.

> **If Teamlab is on your list — it goes on Day 4 or 5.** Teamlab Borderless has reopened in Azabudai Hills (Roppongi-adjacent) and runs perfectly in the midday red zone. Book now — weeks in advance is standard in summer.

This is also the day for your **best dinner reservation.** Whatever that means to your group — the omakase counter, the Michelin spot. Lock it here.

---

### Day 5 — Jul 19 (Sun): Prime Day #2
**Energy: 🟢 Full capacity — precision placement**

Use this second peak day for something structurally different from Day 4 — so you're not doing two back-to-back "go hard" days in the same mode.

**Options depending on what your group wants:**
- **A day trip** (Kamakura, Hakone) — Kamakura's coastal breeze makes it the heat-safest option; Hakone's elevation is cooler but logistically more involved. Both require early departure (8:00–8:30 AM train)
- **Akihabara + Jimbocho corridor** — very different energy from west-side Tokyo, strong AC infrastructure, excellent on a Sunday when west-side neighborhoods peak on crowds
- **Yanaka/Nezu** — old-Tokyo neighborhood feel, best in the morning, pair with an afternoon at Tokyo National Museum in Ueno

---

### Day 6 — Jul 20 (Mon): The Museum Trap
**Energy: 🟡 Strong but selective**

**Critical flag:** Many Tokyo museums are closed on Mondays. If your Day 6 plan involves a specific museum, verify its Monday schedule *now*. The Mori Art Museum (Roppongi) is open Mondays. Teamlab venues are open. Many smaller museums are not.

**Structural recommendation: Shopping and depachika day — Ginza/Nihonbashi/Marunouchi.** This serves two purposes: it's all indoors, it's close to your hotel (low-transit day when you're starting to tire), and it handles souvenir/gift buying before the final night when you're packing. Mitsukoshi Ginza, Itoya stationery, Ginza SIX — all excellent hot-afternoon territory.

If the group has remaining energy, **Roppongi in the evening** (Mori Art Museum closes late, strong dinner options) works well.

---

### Day 7 — Jul 21 (Tue): The Comfortable Last Day
**Energy: 🟠 Don't overcommit**

Everyone will feel the trip in their legs. Don't introduce a new ambitious neighborhood.

**Structural logic: Stay close, go deep.**

The area around your hotel — Ginza, Tsukiji, Shiodome, **Hamarikyu Gardens** (8-minute walk from Shintomicho, one of Tokyo's finest traditional gardens, morning only) — has more than enough to fill a final full day. Hamarikyu in the 8–10 AM window is genuinely special: a Edo-period garden with a view of the modern skyline, and almost no one knows to go there.

Afternoon: final Ginza depachika sweep, packing, comfortable dinner near the hotel. **Book this dinner in advance** — you want your last meal to land well, and you don't want to be wandering for a table while tired.

---

### Day 8 — Jul 22 (Wed): The Exit
**Energy: 🔴 Logistics**

**Narita timing math for a 6:00 PM departure:**

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| International check-in closes | 4:30 PM (90 min before departure) |
| NRT immigration + security | Arrive NRT by **3:30 PM** |
| Narita Express from Shimbashi | ~60 min → depart Shimbashi by **2:30 PM** |
| Walk/taxi from hotel to Shimbashi | ~15 min → **leave hotel by 2:00–2:15 PM** |

This gives you a usable morning window of roughly **8:00 AM – 12:30 PM.**

**The move:** Tsukiji one last time for breakfast, a final walk through Ginza for any last-minute shopping, back to hotel by noon to pack and close out. Luggage storage at the hotel until departure. Mitsukoshi Ginza basement opens at 10 AM — excellent for final omiyage (souvenir food gifts) in air conditioning.

Do not schedule anything requiring a train ride on departure morning.

## Neighborhood Sequencing Logic

From your Ginza base, sequenced to keep geographic flow coherent and minimize backtracking:

```
Days 1–2:  LOCAL     → Tsukiji, Ginza, walkable (zero transit energy)
Day 3:     NORTHEAST → Asakusa, Ueno (direct Hibiya Line from hotel)
Day 4:     WEST      → Harajuku, Omotesando, Shibuya
Day 5:     VARIABLE  → Akihabara (east, easy), Yanaka (north), or day trip
Day 6:     LOCAL     → Ginza / Nihonbashi / Roppongi (recovery + shopping)
Day 7:     LOCAL     → Hamarikyu, Tsukiji, Ginza farewell
```

This moves: **local → northeast → west → variable → local.** You're never zigzagging across the city on consecutive days, and you return to your neighborhood on the days when you need it most.
