## Must-Do Tourist Landmarks — Done Right

### Senso-ji Temple + Nakamise-dori — Asakusa
**Best time:** 6:00–8:30 AM · **Duration:** 1.5–2 hrs

The most-visited temple in Japan, which means if you go at 10 AM in July you're in a slow-moving crowd in a 95°F heat index. Go at 6. The main gate and pagoda in the early light with almost no one around is genuinely one of the better experiences this city offers, and the surrounding streets — Nakamise still closed, but the side alleys open — are completely different. Take the Asakusa Line or Ginza Line from Higashi-Ginza (two stops). The mother will be comfortable at 6 AM; by the time crowds thicken you're already on your way out.

> **Caveat:** Temple grounds are always open; shops start around 9–10.

---

### Shibuya Crossing + Shibuya Sky Observatory — Shibuya
**Best time:** Observatory at sunset (6:30–7:30 PM) / Crossing at 8–9 PM · **Duration:** 2–3 hrs combined

The crossing during the day is underwhelming — you're just crossing a busy street. At night, with the screens blazing and the sheer volume of people, it earns its reputation. Shibuya Sky (rooftop observatory, Scramble Square building) is the best elevated view in the city right now — it's fully open-air, which makes it a sunset/dusk activity. In July that means 6:30–7:30 PM. Book Shibuya Sky in advance online; it sells out. Observatory at dusk → crossing at night → dinner in Shibuya makes a strong evening block.

> **Caveat:** Shibuya Sky is ¥2,000/person. Worth it. The indoor Shibuya Scramble Square observation floor is also solid if someone has heat fatigue and wants glass between them and the sky.

---

### Meiji Shrine + Yoyogi Park — Harajuku/Shibuya
**Best time:** 7:00–9:30 AM · **Duration:** 1.5–2 hrs

The approach path (the forested gravel walk, not the shopping street) is legitimately shaded and noticeably cooler than the street. The shrine itself is simple — this isn't a spectacle, it's atmosphere. Right after, you have two options: exit toward Takeshita-dori (Harajuku's shopping street, good for the sister) which opens around 10–11, or walk through Yoyogi Park. The park in early morning has joggers, dog walkers, and an almost complete absence of tourists. Good combo day: Meiji → Yoyogi stroll → Takeshita/Omotesando shopping as things open → retreat to Omotesando Hills or Laforet for AC by 11.

> **Caveat:** ¥1,000 if you want to enter the inner shrine area (optional).

---

### Tsukiji Outer Market — Chuo-ku *(walk from hotel)*
**Best time:** 7:30–10:30 AM on a weekday · **Duration:** 1–2 hrs

Literally walking distance from your hotel. The inner market moved to Toyosu in 2018, but the outer market — food stalls, tamagoyaki shops, knife vendors, covered alleys — stayed and is still excellent. Morning is when it's alive. Get tamagoyaki (a rolled egg vendor will let you watch and sample), grilled scallops from one of the seafood stalls, and fresh tuna on rice if you want it at 8 AM. The mother will find this manageable — it's largely covered, short walking distances, lots of places to stop and eat.

> **Caveat:** Some vendors close on Wednesday. Tuesday, Thursday, or Friday are the sweet spots.

---

### Hamarikyu Gardens — Chuo-ku *(near hotel)*
**Best time:** 8:00–10:00 AM or 5:00–6:30 PM · **Duration:** 1–1.5 hrs

A 250-year-old tidal garden sitting in the shadow of Shiodome's glass towers — the contrast is surreal and the photos are exceptional. The garden is flat with well-maintained path surfaces and shaded mature trees at the edges. Admission is ¥300. There's a small tea house on the central pond island where you can sit and have matcha. You can also arrive here by Sumida River water bus from Asakusa — see Unique Experiences below.

> **Caveat:** Midday in July is brutal here — it's mostly open. Morning or evening only.

---

### teamLab Planets — Toyosu
**Best time:** First slot of the day (10 AM) or an evening slot · **Duration:** 1.5 hrs

Fully indoors, fully air-conditioned, legitimately impressive immersive digital art. Of the teamLab venues, Planets is the more visceral one — you walk barefoot through water, you're inside the installations rather than just watching them. The sister will love it. Worth the ticket price (¥3,200–3,800/person depending on timing). Book well in advance online; it sells out weeks ahead in July.

> **Caveat:** You remove shoes and wade through a shallow water room. Not appropriate for anyone with serious mobility issues — mother should be fine. Toyosu is about 20 mins from your hotel via metro.

---

### Studio Ghibli Museum — Mitaka (West Tokyo)
**Best time:** 10:00 AM or 12:00 PM entry slot · **Duration:** 2–3 hrs

Hayao Miyazaki designed this himself, and it shows — the building feels like a Ghibli film brought into three dimensions. Ivy-covered walls, spiral staircases, stained glass, hidden rooms, a rooftop garden with a 5-meter Robot Soldier from Castle in the Sky. Inside: original production cels, hand-drawn storyboards, animation process exhibits, a small theater showing exclusive Ghibli short films (not available anywhere else), the Cat Bus room for kids to climb in, and a café. Photography is not allowed inside, which is intentional — Miyazaki wants you to actually look. Best for whoever in the group loves Ghibli; the rest will find it charming even without deep fandom context. Almost entirely indoors and AC — a solid July midday option.

**⚠️ Tickets are the hardest in Tokyo.** Admission is advance booking only — no walk-ups, ever. Tickets go on sale on the 10th of each month at 10 AM Japan time for the following month. For your July visit, that means **June 10 at 10 AM JST** is your window. They can sell out in under ten minutes — set a phone alarm, have the booking page loaded before 10, and move fast. Entry times are 10:00, 12:00, 14:00, or 16:00. Your passport number is required at booking, and the name must match exactly at the door.

- Tickets: ¥1,000/adult (very affordable — the bottleneck is availability, not price)
- Getting there: 30 min from Tokyo Station via JR Chuo Line to Mitaka Station, then a 5-min community bus or a 15-min walk through Inokashira Park (recommended)
- Combine naturally with Kichijoji Station area afterward — good shopping, cafes, and Inokashira Park itself

> **Caveat:** If you miss the June 10 window, check the official site periodically for returns and cancellations. Tour operator packages (Klook etc.) sometimes include tickets at a premium — a legitimate backup if the direct route fails.

### Shinjuku — Shinjuku
**Best time:** Evening, 6 PM onward · **Duration:** 3–4 hrs

Shinjuku is several things at once. Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane) is a tiny alley of yakitori stalls under smoke and red lanterns — eat standing or on tiny stools, order skewers and beer, don't wear anything white. Golden Gai is a labyrinth of micro-bars with 2–6 seats each — some tourist-unfriendly, some very welcoming, worth a drink or two as a wander even if you don't stay long. The new Kabukicho Tower has a rooftop bar and entertainment complex that's more polished and accessible for a larger group.

> **Caveat:** Omoide Yokocho is not air-conditioned. Go after the evening cools off.

## Local Neighborhood Experiences

### Yanaka — Yanesen area (Nippori/Nezu)
**Best time:** 8:00 AM–12:00 PM · **Duration:** 2–3 hrs

Old Tokyo — the kind that survived the 1923 earthquake and WWII firebombing by luck. The cemetery is full of moss and cats. Yanaka Ginza (a traditional shotengai shopping street) is exactly the kind of local daily-life scene that's genuinely rare to find intact. Small shops selling sembei crackers, pickles, tofu, secondhand goods. The streets are mostly narrow and shaded. Very manageable for the mother — flat, slow-paced, and she'll find it interesting. The younger sister may get bored faster here; good opportunity for the group to split. Combine with Nezu Shrine below.

> **Caveat:** Most shops open around 10. Best experienced as a wander, not a checklist.

---

### Nezu Shrine — Bunkyo-ku *(near Yanaka)*
**Best time:** 8:00–9:30 AM · **Duration:** 45 min–1 hr

A smaller torii tunnel walk — a condensed, quieter Fushimi Inari without the climb or the crowds. In July the grounds are lush and largely tourist-free before 10. Combine naturally with a Yanaka morning (10 min walk between the two).

> **Caveat:** The torii path is short — this is a detour within a Yanaka morning, not a standalone destination.

---

### Shimokitazawa — Setagaya
**Best time:** 11 AM–4 PM (shops open late here) · **Duration:** 2–3 hrs

The neighborhood for vintage clothes, small live music venues, independent cafes, and genuinely bohemian-Tokyo energy. Strong pull for the sister and partner. The streets are narrow and shaded by buildings and canvas awnings, with enough cafes and AC shops to make midday survivable. Best vintage shopping in Tokyo — the density of second-hand clothing stores is unmatched. Also has a serious food scene (ramen, curry, coffee).

> **Caveat:** About 20–25 mins from your hotel by metro. Crowds peak on weekends — go on a weekday if you can.

---

### Kagurazaka — Shinjuku-ku
**Best time:** 5:30–9:00 PM · **Duration:** 2–3 hrs

A French-Japanese neighborhood — French bakeries next to traditional kappo restaurants. The hidden alleys behind the main slope are remarkably intact — narrow stone paths, old machiya restaurants with red lanterns, the rare sense of pre-modern Tokyo layered under the modern city. Very photogenic in the evening. Good dinner area. The main slope is manageable for the mother; the alleys are short and flat.

> **Caveat:** This is an evening place. Daytime is fine but it loses its character.

---

### Nakameguro Canal — Meguro
**Best time:** 6:00–9:00 PM · **Duration:** 2 hrs

Famous for cherry blossoms in spring, but the canal walk at dusk in July is underrated — cooler air from the water, good restaurant and bar density, boutique shopping along the banks. The sister and partner will find things to do here (shopping) while the mother can sit comfortably at a canal-side cafe.

> **Caveat:** The boutique shopping here skews expensive. Worth a browse, not a buying destination necessarily.

---

### Kiyosumi-Shirakawa — Koto-ku
**Best time:** 9:00 AM–12:00 PM · **Duration:** 1.5–2 hrs

The neighborhood that Tokyo's third-wave coffee scene is centered on — Blue Bottle Japan's first location is here, along with a cluster of excellent small roasters. Very quiet, very local feel, industrial-warehouse-turned-coffee-shop aesthetic. Near Kiyosumi Garden (small Edo-period garden, very good). Can logically combine with a teamLab day since Toyosu is nearby.

> **Caveat:** The appeal is the vibe and the coffee, not a list of things to do. Not for everyone.

---

### Tsukishima — Chuo-ku *(near hotel)*
**Best time:** 6:00–8:30 PM for dinner · **Duration:** 1.5 hrs

A small island in the Sumida River delta, 15 minutes from your hotel. Known for monjayaki — Tokyo's messier, wetter version of okonomiyaki (savory pancake). There's one street lined almost entirely with monjayaki restaurants, and the experience of sitting at a teppan grill and making your own food in a neighborhood that still feels genuinely local is worth an evening. The group can all participate in the cooking, which makes it social. Very few tourists.

> **Caveat:** The texture is an acquired taste for some — preview monjayaki before committing to the neighborhood.

---

### Togoshi Ginza Shotengai — Shinagawa
**Best time:** 10:00 AM–1:00 PM · **Duration:** 1.5–2 hrs

The longest covered shopping street in Japan — 1.3 km of small, locally-owned shops. Hardware stores, sembei sellers, toy shops, hole-in-the-wall ramen counters, a shoe repair next to a wagashi sweet shop. Almost no tourists. This is daily-life Tokyo as it actually exists, not curated for visitors. Not for everyone — the sister may find it slow, the mother will find it manageable and probably interesting. About 30 minutes south of Ginza by metro.

> **Caveat:** You have to want this kind of experience. If the group is primarily landmark and shopping focused, skip it. If someone wants "real Tokyo," this delivers.

## Unique and Unusual Experiences

### Sumida River Water Bus (Asakusa → Hamarikyu) — River route
**Best time:** Depart Asakusa 9:00 AM · **Duration:** 40 mins one way

A low-key river boat cruise that deposits you at the back gate of Hamarikyu Gardens. The boat passes under bridges, past old warehouses and the Tokyo Skytree reflection. Combine with an Asakusa morning: early Senso-ji → market breakfast → water bus → Hamarikyu. The mother will be very comfortable on the boat — seated, river breeze. Frequency is every 35–45 mins.

> **Caveat:** Not air-conditioned on the river bus — plan for it to be warm on board.

---

### Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building Observatory — Shinjuku
**Best time:** Evening, especially nights open until 10:30 PM (South Tower) · **Duration:** 45 min

Completely free. 202 meters up, clear views over the city. No one talks about this because it's free and unglamorous, but on a clear evening it's one of the best elevated views available. South Observatory is open until 10:30 PM on most nights. Good pre- or post-dinner addition to a Shinjuku evening.

> **Caveat:** Views can be hazy in July due to humidity. Check a forecast for clearer evening windows.

---

### Intermediatheque — Marunouchi (KITTE building, Tokyo Station)
**Best time:** Any midday · **Duration:** 1 hr

A genuinely strange free museum occupying the 2nd and 3rd floors of the KITTE shopping complex — a joint venture between the University of Tokyo and Japan Post. Natural history specimens, old scientific instruments, taxidermied animals, antique design objects, all displayed with no conventional didactics. Cool, quiet, and unlike any other museum experience in the city. The building also has a rooftop terrace with a direct view into Tokyo Station's historic roof.

> **Caveat:** Small. Don't devote more than 1–1.5 hours.

---

### Koishikawa Korakuen Garden — Bunkyo-ku
**Best time:** 8:30–10:30 AM · **Duration:** 1–1.5 hrs

One of Tokyo's oldest surviving Edo-period landscape gardens — established 1629. Smaller than Shinjuku Gyoen and far less visited, sitting in the shadow of the Tokyo Dome. The design is a miniature map of Japanese and Chinese landscapes — specific hills represent specific mountains, specific ponds represent specific lakes. Best garden in the city for the kind of person who wants to understand what they're looking at. Beautiful in July when everything is green.

> **Caveat:** The adjacent Tokyo Dome area is garish by contrast — orient your exit away from it.

---

### Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden — Shinjuku/Sendagaya
**Best time:** 8:30 AM open · **Duration:** 1.5–2 hrs in the morning

The best-maintained garden in Tokyo — French formal, English landscape, and Japanese traditional sections in one park. In July it's all green; the greenhouses provide an AC option mid-visit. Good for a longer morning if the group wants outdoor time before 10:30. ¥500 entry.

> **Caveat:** No food or alcohol allowed inside. It's a contemplative space, not a picnic destination.

## Indoor / AC-Friendly Options

### Ginza Six — Ginza *(5-min walk from hotel)*
**Best time:** 11 AM–3 PM (midday refuge) · **Duration:** 2–3 hrs

The flagship Ginza Six is excellent as a building experience beyond shopping — basement food halls, a Tsutaya bookstore/art space, a rooftop garden, and a Noh theater with occasional public programming. The mother will be completely happy here for hours. The food basement (B2) is one of the best in the city.

> **Caveat:** High-end retail — browsing costs nothing, buying costs a lot.

---

### Isetan Shinjuku — Shinjuku
**Best time:** Midday on a weekday · **Duration:** 2–3 hrs

The best department store in Japan by a meaningful margin — the food halls in the basement are famous among Tokyoites, the cosmetics floor is exceptional, and the curation across all floors is more interesting than a typical department store. If the mother loves malls, she will love Isetan. The sister will find things here too (Isetan has strong streetwear and younger fashion floors).

> **Caveat:** Can get crowded on weekends. Weekday midday is the sweet spot.

---

### KITTE — Marunouchi *(Tokyo Station)*
**Best time:** Midday · **Duration:** 1.5–2 hrs

A converted old post office headquarters right next to Tokyo Station — shopping floors, excellent food hall, and the Intermediatheque museum (see above). The atrium has a free rooftop terrace with the most famous view of Tokyo Station's historic red brick facade. Good if you're transiting through Tokyo Station anyway.

---

### Mori Art Museum + Tokyo City View — Roppongi Hills
**Best time:** Afternoon/evening · **Duration:** 2–3 hrs

The Mori Art Museum (52nd floor) is one of the better contemporary art spaces in the city. The combined ticket includes City View (53rd floor observation), which is excellent on a clear evening. The Roppongi Hills complex itself is large, cool, and has strong food options. ¥2,000/person combined ticket.

> **Caveat:** Check what's currently showing — the specific exhibition matters more here than at some other museums.

---

### Artizon Museum — Nihonbashi
**Best time:** Midday · **Duration:** 1.5–2 hrs

Rebuilt and reopened in 2020 — beautifully renovated, very good Impressionist and post-war Japanese art collection, low crowd levels. Close to Tokyo Station and your hotel. Genuinely underrated compared to the Mori and Tokyo National Museum. Admission ¥1,500–2,200 depending on exhibition.

> **Caveat:** Smaller than the Tokyo National Museum — if someone wants a full-day art deep-dive, go to Ueno.

---

### 21_21 Design Sight — Roppongi (Midtown)
**Best time:** Midday · **Duration:** 1–1.5 hrs

Tadao Ando–designed gallery in Midtown Roppongi, focused entirely on design exhibitions — typically very clever, well-curated. The building itself is worth the trip. Smaller and more niche than the Mori, but consistently excellent depending on the current show. Check online first. The adjacent Midtown complex is good for AC and food.

---

### Tokyo National Museum — Ueno
**Best time:** 9:30 AM opening · **Duration:** Half day (3–4 hrs)

The largest and oldest museum in Japan — Buddhist sculpture, samurai armor, ceramics, textiles, painting. A half-day minimum if anyone cares about Japanese history and art. The Ueno park area also has multiple other museums (Western Art, Science, Metropolitan) if someone wants to extend. Ueno Park itself has Ameyoko market for a street market pass-through.

> **Caveat:** Big museum fatigue is real. The main building is the priority; branch buildings can be skipped.

---

### Akihabara — Chiyoda
**Best time:** Midday · **Duration:** 2–3 hrs

Electronics, anime merchandise, multi-floor arcades, maid cafes, retro game shops. For the sister, this has strong pull. The multi-floor arcades (Club Sega, Taito HEY) have a particular kind of chaotic energy worth experiencing once. Almost entirely indoors and AC. About 15 mins from your hotel via metro.

> **Caveat:** If no one in the group is into anime or retro gaming, an hour is enough.

---

### Department Store Food Halls (Depachika) — Ginza / Shinjuku / Shibuya
**Best time:** Late morning or late afternoon · **Duration:** 1–2 hrs

The basement floors of Mitsukoshi Ginza, Takashimaya Shinjuku, and Shibuya Hikarie all have exceptional prepared food, pastry, bento, confectionery, and produce sections. It's the best place to assemble a picnic or just graze and taste. The mother will love the air conditioning and the presentation; the sister will love the food. Good pre-dinner or lunch alternative that also works as heat refuge.

## Evening / Nightlife (Mixed Group)

### Yurakucho Under the Tracks — Yurakucho *(6-min walk from hotel)*
**Best time:** 6:00–9:30 PM · **Duration:** 2–3 hrs

Almost your doorstep. The arches under the Yamanote Line tracks between Yurakucho and Shimbashi stations have been izakaya bars and restaurants for decades — yakitori smoke, salarymen, beer, very low prices, zero tourists. This is the version of Tokyo evening drinking that actually exists every night for regular people. The mother can sit comfortably; food comes to the table; the noise level is festive without being overwhelming. Start here on a low-key night.

> **Caveat:** These are small, smoky establishments. Not a place for someone with breathing issues.

---

### Golden Gai — Shinjuku
**Best time:** 8:00–11:00 PM · **Duration:** 1.5 hrs

Six alleyways with roughly 200 tiny bars, most with 4–8 seats. Some have a ¥500–1,000 cover charge; many are explicitly welcoming to foreigners now. You don't need to stay anywhere long — the experience is as much the exploration as the drinking. Good for the younger sister; the mother may prefer the outside world after one look. Best as an add-on to a Shinjuku evening, not the centerpiece.

> **Caveat:** Almost no food. Go after dinner.

---

### Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane) — Shinjuku
**Best time:** 7:00–10:00 PM · **Duration:** 1–1.5 hrs

More accessible than Golden Gai for a mixed group — you're sitting at a stall eating skewers, not navigating tiny bars. The atmosphere is smoky and very evocative. The alleys are tight but the mother will manage fine. Order the chicken hearts and cartilage if you're adventurous; chicken thigh if you're not.

> **Caveat:** No room for anyone with claustrophobia. The alleys are genuinely narrow.

---

### Shibuya Crossing at Night — Shibuya
**Best time:** 8:30–9:30 PM · **Duration:** 30–45 min

Best done as a component of a larger Shibuya evening. Stand at the Starbucks second-floor window for the elevated view — it's always packed but you can get a spot if you're positioned early. Then go down and cross it. Cross it multiple times. It earns its reputation at night.

---

### Sumida River Evening Cruise — Departs Hinode Pier
**Best time:** Sunset, roughly 6:30 PM in July · **Duration:** 40–60 mins

Tokyo Cruise operates evening routes — the Skytree and city lights reflecting on the river at dusk is genuinely beautiful. More relaxed than a theme cruise; this is a commuter-style boat that also happens to have a beautiful view. Good for the mother (seated, cooler). Departs from Hinode Pier near Hamarikyu.

---

### High-Floor Bar — Multiple options
**Best time:** 7:00–9:30 PM · **Duration:** 1.5–2 hrs

A few genuine options at different price points:

- **New York Bar, Park Hyatt Shinjuku** — ¥2,000 cover, live jazz, the Lost in Translation one. Legitimately special as an occasion.
- **Andaz Tokyo Bar, Toranomon Hills** — Slightly more contemporary and slightly less crowded. Strong views.
- **Bellustar Tokyo, Kabukicho Tower** — Newer, flashier, more accessible price-wise. Good for a Shinjuku evening splurge.

## Day Trips — Honest Recommendations

### Kamakura — 1 hr from Tokyo Station (Yokosuka Line)
**Recommended · Full day · Depart 7:30 AM**

Great Buddha, Hasedera temple (coastal hillside garden, excellent), Tsurugaoka Hachimangu, and a short walk to the ocean. The sea breeze makes it noticeably more bearable than central Tokyo in July. The temple circuit between Kita-Kamakura and Kamakura is partially shaded. Manageable for the mother if you pace it well — the Great Buddha area is flat; hillside temples have optional stairs. Use your Suica/IC card; this is an easy transit day.

> **Caveat:** Do your outdoor walking before noon and retreat to a restaurant or cafe midday before heading back.

---

### Nikko — 2 hrs from Asakusa (Tobu Limited Express) or Shinjuku (JR)
**Conditional · Full day · Depart 7:00 AM**

The Toshogu shrine complex is one of the most ornate and beautiful things in Japan — deliberately overwhelming in its gold and color, unlike anywhere else. However: it's 2 hours each way, involves significant outdoor walking on uneven terrain, and July is warm even in the mountains. Recommended for Alex specifically given this is a first trip — but consider whether the group can split, or whether the mother finds the day worthwhile vs. tiring.

> **Caveat:** Book the Limited Express tickets in advance. Toshogu entrance area has some stairs.

---

### Hakone — 1.5 hrs from Shinjuku (Romancecar Limited Express)
**Conditional — better as an overnight · Depart mid-morning**

The Open Air Museum (sculptures in outdoor park + indoor galleries) is one of the best museum experiences near Tokyo. The Owakudani volcanic area and ropeway are dramatic but exposed. Fuji views in July are 40/60 at best — clouds often cover the mountain until later in the season. A ryokan overnight (traditional inn, onsen, kaiseki dinner) is a potentially transformative experience — and for the mother, the combination of an AC room, hot spring, and being served an elaborate multi-course dinner without moving is ideal. As a day trip only, it's a long haul for uncertain Fuji views.

- Ryokan price range: ¥15,000–¥60,000+ per person. Mid-range options are perfectly good.

> **Caveat:** If doing this, carve out a night. Day-trip only is not worth the transit time in July.

## DisneySea — Recommended Timing

**Book Thursday July 17 or Friday July 18.**

Weekday crowds before the weekend surge, mid-trip timing so everyone still has energy, and a recovery day still available afterward. DisneySea is outdoor-heavy — that's unavoidable — but it has more indoor rides and AC spaces than Disneyland. July is hot there; arrive at open (8 AM), use Standby Pass + Disney Premier Access strategically, lean into indoor and water attractions midday, and don't try to do everything. It's a full day no matter what.

Book online ahead of time — July is peak season and it fills up. The Fantasy Springs area requires a separate entry strategy; factor this into your planning.

## Spa Day — Partner's Mother (Facial / Mani / Pedi)

The good news: Tokyo has an exceptional beauty and wellness culture, and English-friendly options have expanded significantly. The honest caveat: most Japanese nail and beauty salons operate entirely in Japanese — you need to go in knowing which places have confirmed English support, or you will stand at the door uncertain. All options below are documented English-friendly.

One framing note: a spa morning for the mother pairs naturally with a split-group day. While she gets a facial and nails, the sister can browse Omotesando or Shimokitazawa. You and your partner can use the same window for an onsen visit (below) or a longer neighborhood day. The timing works out well.

---

### Budget Tier (¥3,000–8,000 total for mani + pedi)

**Nail Salon Top Coat — Shibuya**
Specifically built for English-speaking visitors — the entire operation is English-friendly. Japanese nail art (gel, design work) is genuinely world-class, and this is a good entry point for someone wanting the experience without language anxiety. Mani + pedi from approximately ¥5,000–7,000. Book online via their website. Shibuya location makes it easy to combine with other activities in the area.

**Nailsgogo — Shibuya**
Another English-speaking salon near Shibuya and Shinjuku stations. Acrylic nails, gel nails, pedicure, manicure, foot spa, and ingrown toenail care. Open 10 AM–10 PM, which is useful for flexibility. Located in Shibuya Center Building. Instagram: @nailsgogo for designs. ¥2,999–5,000 depending on complexity.

**Aspire Nails — Omotesando**
Run by a Japan Nailist Association Master Educator — the quality of design work here is exceptional. Located walking distance from Omotesando Station, so pairs naturally with shopping or the area's cafes. Pedicures, foot scrubs, and massages also available. Book online. Slightly higher price point for the quality: ¥5,000–9,000.

---

### Mid-Range Tier (¥10,000–25,000 for facial + nails)

**Elana Jade Beauty Spa — Hiroo/Azabu**
The most comprehensive English-speaking full-service salon in Tokyo — facials, massage, nails, lashes, brows, and waxing all under one roof, specifically marketed to international visitors. The mother could do a facial and manicure in a single appointment. Pricing is mid-range for Tokyo luxury standards: facials from ¥8,000–15,000, nails from ¥5,000. Book through their website with English booking options.

**Boudoir Day Spa — Azabu Juban, Minato-ku**
Multilingual day spa in operation since 1999 — one of the longest-running English-friendly beauty destinations in Tokyo. Shellac manicures and pedicures (including a free take-off kit), facials, waxing, spray tanning, and eyelash extensions. Well-organized and reliable. Mani + pedi combined ¥8,000–12,000. Facial from ¥10,000. Azabu Juban is a pleasant neighborhood to be in for the surrounding time.

**Mocci Beauty — Azabu Juban**
English-friendly skin clinic specifically praised for Japanese facial technique — intensive skin treatments plus relaxation therapies. Good for someone interested in a dedicated facial rather than a combined beauty service. ¥10,000–20,000 depending on treatment. Same neighborhood as Boudoir, so the mother could theoretically do nails at one and a facial at the other in the same half-day.

---

### Luxury Tier (¥25,000–60,000+ for full spa day)

**The Peninsula Tokyo Spa — Marunouchi (near hotel)**
The most accessible luxury spa relative to your base — the Peninsula is a 10-minute taxi from your hotel. ## Onsen for Alex + Partner — What You Need to Know

**The key rule first:** Tokyo imposes a complete ban on day-use couple onsen, so even married couples cannot bathe together. This is Tokyo-specific. The traditional onsen experience in Japan is gender-separated — men go to the men's bath, women go to the women's bath, and you meet in the common areas and lounges. This is normal, not a compromise. The actual bathing is a solo/same-gender experience; the social element happens in the yukata lounge before and after.

For a genuine mixed-gender shared experience, your options are: (a) a private rental room within a city facility, or (b) a ryokan with a private in-room or reservable onsen — which is the proper version and ties directly to the overnight trips below.

---

### In Tokyo — Gender-Separated (Traditional Experience)

**Thermae-Yu — Kabukicho, Shinjuku**
*Best for: Evening after a Shinjuku day · Duration: 2–3 hrs · Cost: ¥2,860–3,300/person*

Located in Kabukicho, Shinjuku, Thermae-Yu operates 24 hours a day with multiple indoor and outdoor baths, saunas, relaxation lounges, and dining. The water is delivered daily from Izu, an area famous for its natural volcanic springs, to fill the rotenburo (open-air baths). This is the best city-centre option for a late evening wind-down — go after a Shinjuku dinner, spend 2–3 hours, meet in the co-ed lounge area in your provided yukata for a drink and food. The experience of sitting in a yukata in a manga lounge with your partner after a soak is very much the point. Towels and loungewear included in admission.

> **Caveat:** Tattoo policy — many onsen prohibit tattoos. Confirm current Thermae-Yu policy at booking time if relevant.

**Spa LaQua — Tokyo Dome City, Bunkyo**
*Best for: Afternoon into evening · Duration: 3–4 hrs · Cost: ¥2,750–3,300/person*

Spa LaQua features natural hot spring water pumped from 1,700 meters underground — genuine onsen water, not transported. LaQua offers a selection of beauty treatments including Thai massages, aromatherapy head spa treatments and Korean body scrubs, and recently introduced an outdoor cocktail lounge with a foot pool. More resort-feeling than Thermae-Yu, slightly more expensive, better daytime atmosphere. The co-ed lounge areas are more spacious here. Good option if you want to pair the onsen with a Tokyo Dome City afternoon.

**Toyosu Manyo Club — Toyosu (near teamLab)**
*Best for: Daytime · Duration: 2–4 hrs · Cost: ~¥2,500–3,000/person*

Spring water from Hakone Onsen and Yugawara Onsen is shipped in daily by tanker truck, and the bathhouse is open 24 hours. The rooftop foot bath has views of Tokyo Bay. Opened February 2024 — new, clean, and uncrowded relative to more established facilities. The location near Toyosu makes it a natural pairing with a teamLab Planets visit: do teamLab in the afternoon, Manyo Club in the evening. The foot bath area is co-ed and genuinely relaxing.

**Kudochi Onsen — Roppongi**

### Option 1 — Hakone (1 night) · The Classic Choice
**Transit:** Romancecar Limited Express from Shinjuku → Hakone-Yumoto, 85 min · ¥2,470/person each way · Book in advance

**Depart:** 12:30–1:00 PM from Shinjuku Station
**Return:** 3:00–4:00 PM the following day

**Why it works:** Closest, most seamless, and the ryokan-onsen-kaiseki combination is exactly what a first-time visitor should experience once. The Romancecar is a designated scenic express — it has assigned seats, large windows, and a cafe car. The journey itself is part of the experience.

**Saturday afternoon:**
Arrive Hakone-Yumoto ~3 PM. Check in to ryokan — most will hold your bags for a later check-in. Take the Hakone Tozan Railway up the mountain (it zigzags up in switchbacks — brief but memorable) to Gora Station, then the funicular to Koen-kami for the **Hakone Open Air Museum**. This is one of the best museum experiences near Tokyo: outdoor sculpture park with Picasso pavilion, Henry Moore pieces, Chagall mosaics, indoor contemporary galleries, and a foot bath mid-park where you soak your feet in natural hot spring water between sculptures. Manageable in the afternoon even in July — the sculpture grounds are partially shaded, and the indoor galleries provide AC. ¥1,600/person. 2 hrs.

**Saturday evening:**
Return to ryokan by 6 PM. The sequence from here: private or communal onsen bath, then yukata, then kaiseki dinner served in your room or the inn's dining room. This is the full traditional experience — small, sequential courses of seasonal Japanese cuisine, sake, unhurried service. The bath before dinner is not optional; it's the architecture of the evening.

**Sunday morning:**
Second onsen bath (morning is often the best — cooler air, quieter facility). Ryokan breakfast is typically Japanese: grilled fish, rice, miso, pickles, tamagoyaki. Check out by 11 AM. Take the ropeway to **Owakudani** volcanic valley — sulfurous steam vents, black eggs cooked in the volcanic water (legend says each one adds 7 years to your life), and the best available Fuji views on clear days. Return via ropeway, bus, and train back to Hakone-Yumoto, on the Romancecar home.

**Accommodation:**
- **Gora Kadan** (¥35,000–55,000/person) — former imperial family villa, the best in the area, exceptional kaiseki, full private onsen option. Book 2–3 months ahead.
- **Hakone Ginyu** (¥25,000–35,000/person) — all rooms have private open-air baths and terrace views of the gorge. Fills quickly.
- **Hatsuhana** (¥15,000–22,000/person) — reliable mid-range, good food, proper onsen, no unnecessary frills. Solid value.

**What mom + sister do:**
Saturday: Full Odaiba day (DiverCity, Aqua City, TeamLab Borderless if not already done) or Sunshine City/Ikebukuro with Pokémon Center. Both are full-day activities requiring no support. Sunday: Self-directed Ginza and Nihonbashi morning — Mitsukoshi, depachika grazing, Ginza Six — or a slow morning at the hotel and a Tsukiji breakfast walk.

### Option 2 — Kyoto (1–2 nights) · The First-Timer's Case
**Transit:** Shinkansen Nozomi from Tokyo Station → Kyoto Station, 2 hrs 15 min · ¥13,850/person each way · Reserve seats in advance

**Depart:** 8:00–9:00 AM from Tokyo Station
**Return (1 night):** Sunday evening, 5:00–7:00 PM Shinkansen home
**Return (2 nights):** Monday early afternoon

**Why it works:** This is the most culturally significant destination in Japan and you are a first-timer. Nara, Osaka, Hiroshima — those can wait for a second trip. Kyoto cannot. The combination of Fushimi Inari at dawn, Gion in the evening, and a ryokan night is a complete Japan experience in 36 hours. It is also completely doable — Tokyo to Kyoto is 2 hours 15 minutes, not a major expedition.

**Day 1 — Saturday:**
Arrive Kyoto ~11 AM. Check bags at the station (coin lockers or hotel), head straight to **Fushimi Inari Taisha** — the 10,000-torii gate mountain. The Instagram base is beautiful but you should walk at least 45 minutes up to the mid-mountain for the full experience: the crowds thin dramatically after the first 20 minutes, the gates become denser and more atmospheric, and the views over Kyoto emerge. This is one of those experiences that delivers far beyond its reputation if you do it right. Do it before 1 PM to avoid the worst heat. Take a taxi or the JR Nara Line from Kyoto Station (10 min).

Check into ryokan/hotel in Gion district. Afternoon: walk the **Gion district** — Hanamikoji Street with its preserved machiya townhouses and occasional glimpse of geiko (geisha) in transit, the covered Nishiki Market ("Kyoto's kitchen") for grazing, Yasaka Shrine at the end of the avenue. Evening: kaiseki dinner in Gion. The concentration of excellent restaurants here is unmatched in Japan — this is the heartland of Japanese haute cuisine. Budget ¥10,000–25,000/person for dinner.

**Day 2 — Sunday:**
Morning: **Philosopher's Path** — a 2 km stone-paved canal walk between Nanzen-ji and Ginkaku-ji (Silver Pavilion). Early morning in July means it's relatively cool, lush, and quiet. The small cafes and galleries along the path make it pleasant to pause. Follow with **Nanzen-ji** temple and its aqueduct (very photogenic, almost no tourists at 7–8 AM). Then either: transit to **Arashiyama** (bamboo grove + Tenryu-ji garden — 30 min by bus or Sagano Scenic Railway) for the morning, or take the Shinkansen home from Kyoto Station mid-morning if doing 1 night only.

**Accommodation in Kyoto:**
- **Gion Hatanaka** (¥40,000–70,000/person) — the most authentic ryokan experience in Gion, minimal rooms, exceptional kaiseki, possibility of geiko dinner entertainment. Book months ahead.
- **The Blossom Kyoto or Setre Higashiyama** (¥15,000–25,000/person) — boutique hotels in or near Gion with modern Japanese design, good service, no meal service but excellent location for restaurant access
- **Budget option: Piece Hostel Gion** (¥5,000–8,000/person for private room) — clean, well-run, very central in Gion. No frills but excellent location.

> **Caveat:** July Kyoto is hot — comparable to Tokyo or hotter. Identical heat strategy: outdoor activity before 10 AM and after 5 PM, covered market and indoor temples midday.

**What mom + sister do (1 or 2 nights alone):**
Night 1: They're in the hotel, comfortable, independent. Sister can explore Shibuya or Shimokitazawa in the evening. Mother rests or does an easy Ginza Six dinner.
Full day if 2 nights away: La Qua spa day (see split group section) — this is the ideal time for it. Mother gets the full onsen/wellness experience at La Qua while sister explores Tokyo Dome City. Or: Skytree + Solamachi full day. Or simply: Isetan Shinjuku + Omoide Yokocho evening for the sister.

### Option 3 — Atami, Izu Coast (1 night) · Lowest Effort, Pure Ryokan
**Transit:** Shinkansen Kodama or Hikari from Tokyo Station → Atami, 45–50 min · ¥3,760/person each way · No advance booking required (Kodama is rarely full)

**Depart:** 1:00–2:00 PM from Tokyo Station

Morning bath. Japanese breakfast. Brief walk through old Atami town to see the fish market street and the MOA Art Museum (15 min taxi from town) — the MOA is a seriously good museum, an extraordinary hilltop building with a Rinpa collection, Noh theater, and panoramic views over the Pacific. ¥1,600/person, 1.5 hrs. **What mom + sister do:**

### Option 4 — Nikko (1 night) · Sightseeing Focused
**Transit:** Tobu Nikko Limited Express from Asakusa → Nikko, 2 hrs · ¥1,390/person each way (book in advance) or JR options via Utsunomiya

**Depart:** 8:30–9:00 AM from Asakusa
**Return:** by 5:00 PM the following day

**Why it works:** If the priority is a sightseeing overnight rather than a relaxation overnight, Nikko is the call. The Toshogu shrine complex is one of the most visually overwhelming places in Japan — deliberately so, covering every surface in gold lacquer, carved animals, and intricate color. This is less romantic and more "genuinely extraordinary place you should see on a first trip."

**Day 1:**
Arrive Nikko ~11 AM. The town is compact and walkable. Check in to onsen ryokan in the Nikko or Kinugawa Onsen area (see below). Evening bath and dinner as above.

**Accommodation:**

- **Nikko Kanaya Hotel** (¥15,000–25,000/person) — the historic hotel of Nikko, opened 1873, a remarkable old building with Western-Japanese hybrid architecture. Restaurant is genuinely good. No full onsen, but a classic property.
- **Kinugawa Onsen ryokan** (30 min further by train, ¥15,000–30,000/person) — if you want the full ryokan+onsen night, these resorts in the adjacent Kinugawa valley have better onsen facilities than Nikko proper.

The same self-directed Tokyo day — by this point in the trip they'll have their bearings and can manage a full day independently without any planning from you.

### Hamacho + Nihonbashi Neighborhood Walk — Chuo-ku *(near hotel)*
**Best time:** 9:00–11:30 AM · **Duration:** 2 hrs

## Onsen for Alex + Partner

First, a genuine structural note because this is something most guides bury: **traditional Japanese onsen are gender-separated.** The men's and women's baths are entirely separate spaces. You soak on your side, your partner soaks on theirs, and you reconvene in the locker room or rest area afterward. This is the standard experience and it's worthwhile on its own terms — but if you want to soak *together*, what you're looking for is a **kashikiri-buro** (貸切風呂): a private reserved onsen bath that a couple or small group books exclusively for 45–60 minutes.

**Tattoo policy:** This is a real variable. Traditional onsen in Japan have historically banned visible tattoos (irezumi) due to their historical association with organized crime. In 2025 Tokyo, the policy is changing but inconsistent — roughly divided into "fully tattoo-friendly," "cover small tattoos with waterproof patches," and "no tattoos at all." I've flagged this below for each option. If either of you has tattoos, the private kashikiri option eliminates the problem entirely — staff don't see you undressed.

---

### In-City Onsen Options

**Daikoku-yu — Sumida** *(near Asakusa, tattoo-friendly)*
A traditional neighborhood sento that is fully tattoo-friendly and one of the most recommended by the tattooed traveler community. Real onsen water (not heated tap), an outdoor rotenburo bath (rare in Tokyo), sauna, carbonated bath, medicinal baths. Old interior, high credibility, genuine atmosphere. Entry is very affordable (standard sento pricing ~¥500–800). Combine with an Asakusa morning — it's 10 min walk from Senso-ji. Gender-separated; no kashikiri. Best visited in the late afternoon or evening when water temperature is most comfortable.

**Kogane-yu — Koenji** *(tattoo-friendly, recently renovated)*
A neighborhood sento with about 90 years of history in Koenji — Tokyo's bohemian music neighborhood — recently renovated into a modern facility while retaining traditional character. Multiple bath types including herb-infused kampo medicinal baths and a pink-water bath. Tattoo-friendly, good sauna, genuine atmosphere. About 25 min from your hotel. Gender-separated; combine with the Koenji vintage shopping day.

**Bunkayokusen — Meguro/Ikejiri-Ohashi** *(tattoo-friendly, outdoor bath)*
Clean, modern, well-maintained sento with the unusual feature of an outdoor rotenburo that permits tattoos — outdoor baths that allow ink are genuinely rare in Tokyo. Two floors with different bath configurations, separate rest space for post-soak chilling, good sauna. Gender-separated. ~¥800 entry plus optional towel rental. About 20 min from your hotel.

**Spa LaQua — Bunkyo (Tokyo Dome City)** *(no tattoos permitted in public areas; kashikiri available)*
The largest and most tourist-accessible onsen facility in central Tokyo — natural hot spring water from underground, multiple indoor and outdoor baths, relaxation lounges, restaurants, and a good sauna. The mother was already flagged this as a possible spa option above. For you two: the facility has private kashikiri bath rooms available for booking, which sidestep any tattoo issue entirely. Entry from ¥2,900/person for day use; kashikiri rooms are an additional fee (~¥3,000–5,000 for the room). Book ahead online for the private bath. Fully AC throughout.

### For the Genuine Onsen Experience: Day-Use Ryokan Kashikiri

If you want the real thing — mineral hot spring water, a beautiful hinoki cypress bath, ryokan-quality setting — and want to soak together in private, several traditional inns near Tokyo offer **day-use kashikiri packages** without requiring an overnight stay. A few strong options:

**Tenzan Tohji-Kyo — Hakone-Yumoto** *(90 min from Tokyo)*
A well-regarded onsen facility in Hakone that has both public (gender-separated) and private kashikiri baths. Entry is ¥300 for public access. Private baths available for couples with advance booking — the setting is in a forested river canyon and the water is genuine volcanic mineral spring. Tattoo policy is somewhat inconsistent for the public baths (small tattoos sometimes permitted, large tattoos sometimes not); the private bath eliminates this entirely. Can combine with a Hakone day trip without staying overnight.

**Hakone Yuryo — Hakone-Yumoto** *(90 min from Tokyo)*
A purpose-built day-use onsen facility specifically designed for couples and families wanting private baths. Multiple kashikiri rooms bookable in advance online (English booking available). Beautiful hinoki and stone bath rooms, some with open-air garden views. Entry is more expensive than a neighborhood sento (~¥1,500–3,000/person plus the private room fee) but the facility is excellent and the English accessibility is high. Book well in advance — private rooms fill up on weekends.
